# Exemplars — the copy technique library

The concrete moves that turn a brief into a page. Harvested from 18 homepage teardowns and 65 repositioning cases, distilled into reusable formulas — patterns, not lifted copy. Use at copywriting time (create Stage 2) and as the "here's the fix" rewrites in an audit.

Formulas use `[brackets]` for the slot to fill. Short illustrative fragments are drawn from real teardowns to show the shape.

## Hero formulas

The hero must pass the 5-second test (see [frameworks.md](frameworks.md#8)). Proven shapes:

- **Without-the-cost:** `[Do the valuable thing] — without [the scary switching cost / painful dependency]`. Defuses the #1 objection in the headline. *("Unify your SIEM and data lake — without replacing Splunk"; "Automate sales workflows without bugging your dev team.")*
- **The only-X-that-Y:** `The only [category] that [unique differentiator]`. Claims category *and* wedge in one line. *("The only enterprise search engine that keeps your data in your cloud.")*
- **Category-anchor with qualifier:** `[Product] is a [category] built specifically for [narrow use case]`. Best when the category is known but you own a slice. *("…built *specifically* for in-person training.")*
- **Already-done:** `[All the specific things] you need. Already done.` For "we removed the work" products. *(Apideck, accounting integrations.)*
- **Switch-it-on:** `[Buyer's verb] [the outcome your customer wants] inside your [product]`. Recasts infrastructure as a capability the buyer turns on. *("Offer loans to your customers inside your platform.")*
- **Finally:** `Finally, a way to [do the hard job] reliably`. "Finally" presupposes a long-unmet need; good when there's no named competitor. *(Root Signals.)*
- **Relatable truism:** a plain, felt statement of the pain. *("Finding a job shouldn't be a full-time job.")*
- **Costly-mistake:** `[Doing the status-quo thing] is a mistake you can't afford`. *("Hiring a VP of Sales too early…")*
- **Credibility-anchored (personal/solo):** lead with the track record. *("Get a top-of-funnel content strategy — I built the playbooks at [known companies].")*
- **One-platform-to-run:** `One platform to run your [business]`, subhead naming the chaos it replaces. *(AgencyBloc: "…instead of the chaos of spreadsheets and siloed systems.")*

**H1/H2 split:** H1 = the clear job-to-be-done (short, plain, no jargon). H2 = process + differentiation + a credibility marker ("helped 500+ …"). Strip any "infrastructure/platform" abstraction out of the H1.

## Problem-section structures

Every strong "after" adds or sharpens a problem section — it's where the visitor decides "I'm in the right place." Shapes:

- **"[Category] is no place for generic [tool]"** + three named failure modes of the status quo. *(Anterior vs. horizontal AI.)*
- **"You want [goal], but [existing approaches] don't fit because…"** — three ✗ risk cards. *(Atolio.)*
- **"You can't [X] in [incumbent] — and it's [risk to you]."** Names the gap the incumbent leaves. *(Anvilogic vs. Splunk.)*
- **"You've tried [X], but [why it falls short]"** — a triad pre-disqualifying the three common status-quo attempts, so the reader recognizes themselves. *(Root Signals: open-source evaluators, basic guardrails, "vibe checks.")*
- **Staccato cascade** — short escalating fragments dramatizing the status-quo wait, ending on a hard turn: *"…The sales team waits. And waits. Until now."* *(Scout.)*
- **Status-quo tax checklist** — an itemized list of the grind the reader feels line by line (hours scrolling, mass-applying, chasing). *(Passive.)*
- **Three-beat trio** — *"Scattered tools. Siloed data. All on your shoulders."* *(AgencyBloc.)*
- **Named pain-card grid** — 3–4 discrete cards, each one concrete pain. *(Administrate.)*
- **"All your choices have a catch"** — `When it comes to [category], all your choices have a catch`, then each alternative and its catch (self-eliminates competitors before you name yourself). *(Grizzle.)*

## Differentiation moves

- **"Without X / With Y" comparison table** against the named incumbent (often a spreadsheet/manual process). *(AG5 vs. Excel.)*
- **Complement, not replace** — name the incumbent and position beside it to defuse switching fear ("without replacing Splunk").
- **Anti-commoditization block** — `What "[category buzzword]" really means`, unpacking a generic claim into specific, checkable mechanisms. Converts an unbelievable buzzword into differentiation. *(Passive on "AI-tailored.")*
- **Add a modifier to a known category** — don't coin a new label (see [frameworks.md](frameworks.md#9)).
- **Three crisp arguments** — distill the edge into exactly three named differentiators, not a feature dump. *(Sierra: integrations, IDX websites, lead prioritization; Tobiko: parsing, state, lineage, virtual test envs.)*

## Capability / how-it-works blocks

- **Verb-led, pain-matched headers** — rename every feature block from a product-suite label to a customer job: *"Take the stress out of commissions processing,"* not *"Commissions Module."* *(AgencyBloc.)*
- **Show the mechanism** — each capability with a real UI shot proving *how*, not just asserting a benefit.
- **Tabbed, stage-labeled walkthrough** — `Analyze → Structure → Respond`, each tab a real step; turns "how it works" into the differentiation argument. *(Altura.)*
- **Sequential narrative** — a step-by-step homepage story where each step is one capability, framed against the cost of doing it yourself. *(Apideck: prebuilt connectors → auth → unified model → logging → real-time.)*

## Packaging / "what you get"

- **Labeled deliverable grid** — show concrete deliverable *types* as a grid, not a services list. *(Grizzle: JTBD content, product-led content, comparison pages, tool roundups.)*
- **Collapse the menu into a narrative** — replace a multi-tier package menu (TIER / TIER+ / TIER PRO) with one clear offer told as "how we do it." *(Amp Creative.)*
- **Named components** — a small set of named parts (coaching / content / community). *(ProductLed.)*
- **One hero offer, rest secondary** — for solo experts, one primary offer with newsletter/course demoted to "other ways I can help." *(The Reeder.)*

## Self-qualification

- **Checkbox pain list** — `Do you have a [problem]?` with a checklist of pains and a "😊 no problem!" out. *(Fletch's own page.)*
- **"This is for you if…"** — an explicit fit statement. *(ProductLed.)*
- **"Is this you?" columns** — 2–3 columns each describing a recognizable current-state. *(Root Signals.)*

## Proof in context

- **Stat tagged to a named customer + role** — *"8 days saved · $20,000 · 37%"*, each attributed. *(AG5, Altura.)*
- **Testimonial beside the claim** — place social proof next to the specific claim it backs, not floated in a lone logo bar. *(Attribuly: per-brand result stats next to the relevant capability.)*
- **Logo bar under the hero** — borrow trust immediately.

## The consultancy / services homepage (reference build)

The user's founding example — a positioning consultancy's own homepage — is the canonical services composition. It's what "package the intangible" looks like end to end. The live structure, section by section:

1. **Hero** — problem-first: `Let's fix your [customer's problem]`. Subhead = what they are + a credibility marker (companies helped). Two CTAs (primary book + "see proof"). Logo grid immediately below.
2. **Service overview** — the named offer (a productized "sprint"), a three-column deliverables list, and a **pricing table tiered by the customer's stage/revenue** (not flat pricing).
3. **Quantified testimonial block** — a few results-carrying quotes (a conversion lift, an ARR jump) with name, title, logo.
4. **Self-qualification** — `Do you have a [problem]?` as a checkbox list of pains, so the wrong-fit visitor screens themselves out.
5. **Deliverables deep-dive** — the exact tangible outputs (e.g. strategies ranked by risk, a short internal deck, a homepage wireframe), each with a visual preview. Makes an intangible service concrete.
6. **Process / timeline** — `Here's exactly what it looks like`: a day-by-day breakdown with time commitments per session. Transparency removes friction and *is* the differentiator.
7. **FAQ** — handles the obvious objections (scope, timeline, what's not included).
8. **Testimonials at scale** — a large wall of short quote + headshot + name + title.
9. **Footer** — nav, the individual people (linked profiles — a services business sells its people), repeated booking CTA.

**The transferable services pattern:** problem-first hero → productize the offer with a name and tiered pricing → make deliverables tangible with previews → self-qualify hard → show the process as proof → stack human proof. This maps onto the five-part anatomy in the annotated reference (Hero / Package / Self-qualification / Deliverables / Process).

## The before→after transformation library

The 65 cases collapse into a small set of repeatable transformations. Nearly every project is one or more of these:

| # | Transformation | Before → After | Cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Narrow the audience** | speaks to many segments → one best-fit ICP | Banxware, Safecube, Later, Frankli, TrueClicks, Sierra |
| T2 | **Anchor one use case** | use-case overload → one core use case front and center | GroupGreeting, Instruqt, Ripe, Membership, Vero |
| T3 | **Name the competitive alternative** | no enemy → positioned against incumbent/DIY/status quo | Anvilogic (Splunk), Optise (GA), Tobiko (DBT), Rosie (DIY calls), AG5 (Excel), RampMetrics (homegrown) |
| T4 | **Add a problem section** | jumps to features/benefits → dedicated problem the ICP feels | Administrate, Kolleno, Pencil Spaces, Scout |
| T5 | **Show the "how"** | benefit/outcome only → explains the mechanism | Bloomfilter, Ripe, Voltaiq, UserEvidence, Waratek |
| T6 | **Lead with differentiation** | differentiation buried → wedge in the hero | Comeet, Docfield, Synapsa, Vouris |
| T7 | **Category move** | wrong/small category → higher-value or clearer category | Survicate (survey → feedback platform), Zeda, CommandBar (digital adoption), Upslide (horizontal → vertical) |
| T8 | **De-jargon / de-vision** | investor/visionary/jargon copy → plain, product-clear | Mo, SlimAI, Polser, BlinkMetrics, AgencyBloc |
| T9 | **Make it scannable** | wall of text/features → prioritized, skimmable | Blue Triangle, Superhuman, Sprocket 365, ProductLed |
| T10 | **Fix the audience target** | aimed at the wrong buyer → the real buyer/owner | Pressac (owners → resellers), LoneScale (no owner → clear owner), Comeet (hiring managers) |

When auditing, name the transformation(s) the page needs; when creating, these are the levers the brief pulls.

## Teaching devices (for talking a founder into narrowing)

Founders resist narrowing ("we don't want to pigeonhole ourselves"). These analogies do the persuading — reach for one when the strategy is right but the buy-in isn't.

- **The retail shelf** — the software market is a store; buyers shop by aisle (category). The **shelf you stand on determines who you're compared against** (put yourself by Asana and you fight Asana). Invent your own shelf ("Intelligence OS") and you compete with no one — *but nobody walks down that aisle.* Use to explain category choice.
- **The Frankenstein buyer** — if your homepage targets project-managers *and* CTOs *and* marketers, it's speaking to a stitched-together three-headed buyer who doesn't exist. Nobody shops for "legal intake software, a database, AND a social planner." Use to prove a multi-audience message reaches no one.
- **"A home for everyone"** — you build one model home, and every neighborhood wants a tweak (add a gothic wing, a glass tower, an apartment block). Bolt them all on and you get a monstrosity nobody can name, so you slap on a vague tagline — "a home for everyone" — that's clear to no one. Use to explain why absorbing every buyer's demand into one page destroys it. (Real-world tells: "One platform. Endless solutions." / "Put YES to work." / "Making smart smarter.")
- **"It could be anything"** — show a logo, ask "what is this?" A shoe brand gets "SOCKS!" instantly; an abstract SaaS hero gets "…it could be anything." Software is intangible, so the hero must *name* a primary anchor. Use to justify the 5-second test.

## Named hero examples (anchor → copy)

Real heroes that pass by stating a primary anchor ([anchors.md](anchors.md)):

- **Category anchor** — *Linear:* "The issue tracking tool you'll enjoy using." (names the category + differentiates on experience).
- **Use-case anchor** — *Vanta:* "Vanta simplifies the complex, time-consuming, and tedious process of preparing for a SOC 2 audit." (names the job, positions against the painful status quo).
- **"Only X that Y"** — *Atolio:* "The only enterprise search engine that keeps your data in your cloud." (category + wedge in one line).
- **Problem + differentiation (Unique Value)** — *DinMo:* "Most CDPs are inflexible and lead to vendor lock-in. DinMo is a CDP that's composable." · *Kondo:* "The LinkedIn inbox is annoying and slow. Kondo replaces it with a modern UI…"

**Supporting-argument template** (slides 4–6 of the deck, product sections of the page): *"Unlike other [alternative] that [does X], we [do Y]"* + 1–2 **features that make it believable.** Every argument frames against the alternative. Full structure in [templates.md](templates.md).

## Two worked repositioning cases (end to end)

**BugHerd** (website feedback SaaS) — was positioning two products (bug tracking + website feedback) to two audiences with overlapping arguments → confusing. Mapped three options with thesis+risks; **chose the website-feedback use case** because their stickiest customers had it, even though it meant an *immature* category over saturated "bug tracking." Real competition named as the manual status quo (emails, screenshots, Zoom, spreadsheets), not Jira. Filled the Canvas, shipped "Getting client feedback on websites — made ridiculously easy." Result: **~70% uplift in trial-to-paid, doubled monthly growth in <8 months.** Illustrates: options-with-thesis/risks, ICP-by-use-case, real-competition-is-status-quo, deliberate category choice.

**Freckle** (CRM data enrichment) — original "like Clay, without the learning curve" mis-fit (best users *were* technical; not there for outbound). Mapped three options (head-to-head with Clay / "you don't need a GTM engineer" / "easiest way to enrich CRM data"); **chose the enrichment use case** — underserved segment, white space as Clay moved away from it. Canvas argument columns (find any data point / work with incomplete data / always-on) became the three homepage sections. Illustrates: document-what's-wrong first, choose on thesis not test, 1:1 problem→argument mapping.
