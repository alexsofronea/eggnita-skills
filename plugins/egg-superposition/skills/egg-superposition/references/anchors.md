# Anchors — the grammar of positioning

The load-bearing primitive under everything else in this skill. Anchors are the reference points that let a buyer classify *what a product is* by grounding the unfamiliar in the familiar. Get the anchors right and the hero, the category, the ICP, and the competition all fall out of them. Get them wrong and no amount of copy polish saves the page.

Every other reference leans on this one: [frameworks.md](frameworks.md) reasons about *which* anchors to pick, [templates.md](templates.md) is a fill-in of anchors + value, [exemplars.md](exemplars.md) shows anchors turned into copy.

## The two kinds of anchor

**Primary anchors** — each one, *on its own*, is a sufficient definition of what the product is. If you state it, the buyer says *"I get it."* There are exactly three:

| Primary anchor | The sentence it completes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Category** | "…is a **[category]**" | "Attio is a **CRM**" |
| **Use case** | "…helps you **[do the job]**" | "Calendly is for **scheduling meetings**" |
| **Competitive alternative** | "…is a **[known thing]**-replacement" | "Slack is an **email replacement**" |

Primary anchors are **not optional**. They're the cognitive entry point that makes every downstream message (benefits, differentiation, ROI) possible. Without one, the buyer has no framework for what the product does, and value statements land on nothing.

**Secondary anchors** — descriptive context that *enriches* positioning but can **never classify the product on its own.** State one alone and the buyer says *"…but what is it? 🤔"*. Three common ones:

| Secondary anchor | Example |
|---|---|
| **Company type** | "…for **small businesses**" |
| **Persona / department** | "…for **salespeople**" |
| **Desired outcome / benefit** | "…**saves you time**" |

Stacking secondaries doesn't fix it: *"helps salespeople in small businesses save time"* → the buyer still has no idea what it **is**. Outcome/benefit language is a secondary anchor — which is exactly why "leads with benefits, never says what it is" is a top failure mode ([patterns.md](patterns.md)).

## The assembly

**Primary + secondary = your one-sentence explanation.** Tag each phrase:

> "FletchPMM is a `[Category:` product marketing consultancy `]` that helps `[Persona:` founders & marketers `]` in `[Company:` B2B software companies `]` `[Use case:` position their products `]`."

Add the problem and differentiation and you have the **elevator pitch**:

> one-sentence explanation **+ Problem Summary + Differentiation Summary** = elevator pitch.

## Unique Value = Problem + Differentiated solution

Anchors say what you *are*; value says why you *win*. The formula:

> **Unique Value = your customer's Problem + your Differentiated way of solving it.**

The move: **link a key problem to ONE chosen primary anchor.** The anchor you pick changes the problem you tell:

- Problem ↔ **category**: "Most CDPs are inflexible and lead to vendor lock-in." → *DinMo is a CDP that is composable, giving you vendor freedom.*
- Problem ↔ **competitive alternative**: "LinkedIn's inbox is annoying and slow." → *Kondo replaces it with a modern UI…*
- Problem ↔ **use case**: "Getting SOC 2 compliant is incredibly time-consuming." → *Vanta automates the process.*

Problem + differentiation = the unique value you deliver. This is the spine of both templates in [templates.md](templates.md).

## Three rules for using anchors well

1. **Pick the right level of clarity — vaguer fails.** Each anchor sits on a vague→specific spectrum; the vaguer you go, the more likely positioning fails.
   - "software company" → **"CRM"**
   - "help you run your business" → **"help you compose emails"**
   - "replace traditional tools" → **"Arc Browser replacement"**
   - Vague reads as *"I don't get it 🤔"*; specific reads as *"I get it 😃."*

2. **Choosing an anchor is choosing a market — and a competitor set, and a TAM.** A *competitive-alternative* anchor ("we're a Clay alternative") only reaches a market that already knows Clay. A *use-case* anchor ("a way to enrich your CRM") reaches everyone who wants that job done, including people who've never heard of Clay. Same product, different anchor, different market. (See the "what you call your software determines your competitors" cascade in [frameworks.md](frameworks.md).)

3. **Layer anchors to sharpen the ICP.** Nest from broad to narrow to target precisely: Use case ("people updating coworkers") → Competitive alternative ("instead of having meetings") → Department ("on a sales team") → Company type ("in small businesses"). Each layer trades reach for resonance.

## How anchors drive the rest of the skill

- **Discovery** ([discovery.md](discovery.md)) is, at its core, finding the primary anchor that contains a real problem.
- **The maturity fork** ([frameworks.md](frameworks.md#1)) is a choice *between* two primary anchors: category vs. use case.
- **The 5-second hero test** ([frameworks.md](frameworks.md#8)) passes exactly when the hero states a primary anchor ("which tool does this replace?" = category/alternative; "which task does this help with?" = use case).
- **The templates** ([templates.md](templates.md)) are structured fill-ins of anchors + Unique Value.
- **ICP** is anchor + layered secondaries, not firmographics ([frameworks.md](frameworks.md#6)).
